## How to use it

Each week has three parts: **watch** (run the thing and step through it), **explain** (answer the
questions out loud, or on paper, *before* looking at anything), and **check** (a challenge that only
passes if the engine did what you said it would). Turn *Ask me to predict before each reveal* on in the
[Playground](/playground) all six weeks: being asked "what happens next?" and being wrong is where most
of the learning is.

The answer to "why?" is almost always one sentence about **pages**: an algorithm is fast because it reads
fewer of them. Keep asking how many pages a thing reads.

## Week 1 — Pages and the buffer pool

**Watch.** Run a point lookup and a full scan in the [Playground](/playground) and follow the *Buffer Pool*
panel. The [first-query](/learn/first-query) and [eviction](/learn/eviction) lessons walk it. Open the
[slotted-page inspector](/tools/page) and find where a row's bytes live. In the
[pool sandbox](/tools/buffer-pool) run the same reference string under LRU and clock, then under the policies
the SQL pipeline never offers.

**Explain.**

- What is a page, and why does a database read and write whole pages rather than rows?
- What does a buffer pool do, and what happens on a miss when every frame is full?
- Walk through LRU and clock sweep. Where do they choose a different victim? Which is cheaper to maintain?
- What is sequential flooding, and how do 2Q, midpoint LRU or scan-resistant policies avoid it?
- What is a dirty page? What does evicting one cost that evicting a clean one does not?
- Why can a page not be evicted while it is pinned?

**Check.** [Force an eviction](/challenges/eviction-storm), then [the dirty eviction](/challenges/dirty-eviction).
The [policy comparison](/compare) finds the query where LRU and clock disagree.

## Week 2 — Indexes

**Watch.** [The B+Tree concept page](/docs/concepts/btree), then the [sandbox](/tools/btree): insert until a
node splits, delete until it merges, and watch the invariants hold. In the Playground run a lookup and a range
scan and read the *Index* panel; step through the [B+Tree split](/learn/btree-split) lesson.

**Explain.**

- Why a B+Tree and not a binary tree, a hash table, or a plain B-tree? What are leaves linked for?
- What is fan-out? How does it set the height, and what does the height cost in page reads?
- What happens on a leaf split? A merge or borrow? How does the root grow and shrink?
- What is an index over a column that *repeats* — how do the entries stay distinct?
- An index on `(a, b)`: which of `a = 1`, `b = 2`, `a = 1 AND b > 5` can use it? Why? (The leftmost-prefix rule.)
- What is a covering index? Why is `SELECT a, b … WHERE a = 1` cheaper with one?
- How does `UNIQUE` work — what does an `INSERT` do that a plain index would not?
- When is an index *worse* than a scan? (In the Custom database, index a column with only two values and read the optimizer's cost note.)
- Building an index over existing data: why is a bulk load faster and smaller than inserting row by row?

**Check.** [Make the planner use the index](/challenges/use-the-index), [a lookup on the big table](/challenges/deep-descent),
[the range leap](/challenges/range-leap), and [the selective end of a skew](/challenges/needle-in-the-skew).
In [Compare](/compare) run *Index vs. sequential scan*, then *Index build*.

## Week 3 — Joins, sorting and aggregation

**Watch.** The four join algorithms forced onto one query in [Compare → JOIN algorithms](/compare); the
[external merge sort sandbox](/tools/sort), trading memory for passes; the
[Grace hash join sandbox](/tools/hash-join), with partitions and spill.

**Explain.**

- Nested-loop, hash, sort-merge, index nested-loop: what does each read, and when does each win?
- Why does an index join beat a hash join with a tiny outer side — and lose with a big one?
- How does an external merge sort work with M pages of memory and N pages of data? How many passes?
- Hash aggregate vs sort aggregate: what is the memory and I/O difference? When is the sorted output free?
- What does the Volcano (iterator) model mean, and what does it cost per row?
- Why does a `LIMIT` above a hash-based `DISTINCT` stop early but not above a sort-based one?

**Check.** [Hash beats loop](/challenges/hash-beats-loop) and [the sort spill](/challenges/sort-spill).

## Week 4 — The optimizer

**Watch.** The [planner sandbox](/tools/planner): switch rewrite rules on and off and watch the plan change.
In the Playground turn on *Show as → Relational algebra* on the plan, and run `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` — the
estimated rows sit beside the actual ones. The [planner lesson](/learn/planner) walks a rewrite.

**Explain.**

- What is a logical plan versus a physical one? Write a query as relational algebra.
- What does predicate pushdown do and why is it always worth it? Which conjuncts can be pushed below a join?
- How does the optimizer decide whether to use an index? What does it assume about selectivity?
- Read an `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`: where do the estimates and the actuals disagree, and why?
- Why can an optimizer choose an index and still lose? (Hint: heap pages per match.)
- What would join *ordering* add, and why is the search space large?

**Check.** [Push the filter down](/challenges/push-it-down) and [two conditions, still a hit](/challenges/and-that-matches).

## Week 5 — Transactions and recovery

**Watch.** Two sessions on one table in the [schedule tool](/tools/schedule): run the dirty-read preset with no
locking, then under row locks, then on versions. The [dirty-read](/learn/dirty-read) and
[snapshots](/learn/snapshots) lessons walk it, and the [isolation matrix](/tools/isolation) shows which level lets
which anomaly through — each cell a run, not a lookup. Then the log: in the [write-ahead log](/tools/wal) switch
the log-before-data rule off and see what a crash leaves behind, and in [crash recovery](/tools/recovery) read
analysis, redo and undo pass by pass. The [log-first](/learn/log-first),
[steal-and-force](/learn/steal-and-force) and [recovery](/learn/recovery) lessons walk those. The
[lock simulator](/tools/locks) shows the lock table on its own, and the
[protocol comparison](/tools/concurrency) runs one schedule under five ways of keeping it correct.

**Explain.**

- ACID: what does each letter cost? Which anomalies does each isolation level allow, and why does a level mean
  "how long a lock is held"?
- Two-phase locking: growing and shrinking phases; why strict 2PL; how deadlocks are found, and who is chosen to
  lose.
- MVCC: what does a reader see, what do writers do to old versions, and what does it cost (dead versions, `VACUUM`)?
- Snapshot isolation is not serializable: construct write skew. What does a serializable check at commit add?
- Timestamp ordering and optimistic validation: when does each abort a transaction, and how much work goes with it?
- Write-ahead logging: why log before the page? What does a commit actually write? What do steal and force each
  cost, and who pays?
- What does a fuzzy checkpoint record, and why does it not shorten redo by itself?
- Analysis, redo, undo: why is history repeated, including the changes of transactions that did not commit? Why is
  undo itself logged?

**Check.** [Read a value that never existed](/challenges/read-the-unreal),
[make A the deadlock victim](/challenges/pick-the-victim),
[commit what no order explains](/challenges/write-skew),
[make recovery redo a commit](/challenges/redo-a-commit) and
[start redo in the middle of the log](/challenges/start-redo-late).

## Week 6 — Put it together

**Do.** Pick a query and narrate it end to end *before* you run it: parse, plan, which rewrite fires, which scan,
how many pages, what the buffer pool does, what comes back. Then run it and see where you were wrong. Use the
[predict lesson](/learn/predict), then make your own: change a table in *Edit data*, add an index in the
Custom database's *Manage tables* (a composite one, a `UNIQUE` one), and predict what changes.

**Mock.** Ask a friend to hand you a query and a table size and to interrupt with "why?". A good answer names
pages, not vibes: "a point lookup on 400 rows is a handful of index levels plus one heap page, so five or six
reads; a scan is a hundred."

## What this will not prepare you for

System-design rounds, distributed databases, the SQL you would write at work beyond the supported
subset, and the behaviour of any particular production database. Treat QueryLens as the place you *watch the
mechanism*, and the answers above as scaffolding for the depth an interviewer will probe.
